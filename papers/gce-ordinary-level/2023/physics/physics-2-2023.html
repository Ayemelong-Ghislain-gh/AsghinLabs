GCE O Level Physics 2023Paper 2

6 pages · physics-2-2023

✅ See answers🤖 Ask AI tutor

Paper © the examining body named on it (GCE Board, MINESEC or its regional delegation). Shown unedited, with permission, to help students revise.

Answers and explanations

1aConductor vs semiconductor
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: A conductor has very many free electrons and conducts easily; a semiconductor has few free charge carriers, so its conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator.

Also accepted: when heated, a conductor's resistance increases, but a semiconductor's resistance decreases (more carriers are freed).

1bn-type vs p-type semiconductors
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: n-type: doped with a pentavalent (group V) element such as phosphorus or arsenic; majority carriers are electrons. p-type: doped with a trivalent (group III) element such as boron, gallium or indium; majority carriers are holes.

A pentavalent atom has one extra electron that becomes free, so electrons dominate. A trivalent atom has one electron missing, which leaves a hole, so holes dominate.

1cIncreasing conductivity of an intrinsic semiconductor
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Raise its temperature (or shine light on it, or dope it).

Heat or light gives electrons enough energy to break free, creating more electron–hole pairs, so more carriers can conduct.

2a(i)Wavefronts after the obstacles (drawing)
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: X: after the boundary, the wavefronts are closer together (shorter wavelength) and change direction, bending towards the normal. Y: after the gap, the wavefronts spread out as curved arcs with the same spacing as before.

At X the waves pass from deep to shallow water, so they slow down: frequency stays the same, wavelength decreases and the direction changes. At Y the waves spread out through the narrow gap and their wavelength does not change. Draw both clearly with a ruler: straight lines for X and curved arcs for Y.

2a(ii)Phenomenon at X
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Refraction.

The waves change speed, and therefore direction, when they move from deep water to shallow water. (Y shows diffraction.)

2b(i)Definition of interference
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Interference is the effect produced when two coherent waves overlap and combine (superpose), giving a resultant wave that is stronger in some places (constructive) and weaker in others (destructive).

This is the principle of superposition: the resultant displacement at a point is the sum of the displacements of the separate waves.

2b(ii)Amplitude after constructive interference
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 8 cm

In constructive interference the waves meet in phase, so the amplitudes add: 4 cm + 4 cm = 8 cm.

3(i)Transformer output voltage
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: V₀ = 15 V

From the diagram: input 240 V a.c., 4000 primary turns, 250 secondary turns. Vs/Vp = Ns/Np, so Vs = 240 × 250 / 4000 = 15 V. (The numbers on figure 2 are printed in a damaged font in the official paper; these are the values it shows.)

3(ii)Current through the 6 Ω resistor
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: I = 2.5 A

I = V / R = 15 V ÷ 6 Ω = 2.5 A.

3(iii)Transformer with direct current
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: No, it will not work normally: there will be no output voltage.

A transformer works by a changing magnetic flux in the core, which induces an e.m.f. in the secondary. Direct current produces a steady flux, so no e.m.f. is induced. The primary coil may also overheat because only its small resistance limits the current.

4ap.d. across R
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 9 V

R and the 15 Ω resistor are in series, so their p.d.s add up to the battery voltage: 12 V − 3 V = 9 V.

4bCurrent in the 15 Ω resistor
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 0.2 A

I = V / R = 3 V ÷ 15 Ω = 0.2 A.

4cResistance of R
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 45 Ω

In series the same current (0.2 A) flows through R, so R = V / I = 9 V ÷ 0.2 A = 45 Ω.

5aLaw of conservation of linear momentum
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: When bodies collide or interact, their total momentum stays constant, provided no external force acts on them.

Total momentum before = total momentum after, for a closed (isolated) system.

5b(i)Total momentum before collision
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 75 000 kg m s⁻¹

Only the moving car has momentum: p = mv = 2500 × 30 = 75 000 kg m s⁻¹. The stationary car has zero momentum.

5b(ii)Common velocity after collision
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 18.75 m s⁻¹ (about 18.8 m s⁻¹)

Momentum is conserved: 75 000 = (2500 + 1500) × v, so v = 75 000 ÷ 4000 = 18.75 m s⁻¹, in the direction of the first car.

6(i)Another method of charging
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Charging by induction (or charging by contact/conduction).

Friction (rubbing) is already given in the question, so name a different method.

6(ii)Charge on the polythene rod
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Negative charge.

When polythene is rubbed with a cloth, electrons move from the cloth to the polythene, so the rod gains electrons.

6(iii)Why a wet cloth does not charge the rod
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Water conducts electricity, so any charge produced leaks away through the wet cloth (and the hand) to the earth.

Charging by friction only works with dry insulators: the charges must stay where they are produced.

6(iv)Giving the sphere a positive charge (induction)
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 1. Bring the negative rod near the sphere without touching: electrons in the sphere are repelled to the far side. 2. Earth the far side (touch it with a finger): the repelled electrons flow to earth. 3. Remove the finger first, then remove the rod. 4. The sphere is left with a deficit of electrons, so it is positively charged.

Draw a diagram for each step: the sphere with + charges near the rod and − charges on the far side, the earth connection, then the sphere with + charges spread evenly over it. The order matters: if the rod is removed before the earth connection, the electrons flow back and the sphere is left uncharged.

7a(i)Newton's second law
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the resultant force acting on it and takes place in the direction of the force.

For a constant mass this gives F = ma (F in N, m in kg, a in m s⁻²).

7a(ii)Experiment: force and acceleration
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Apparatus: trolley, inclined runway, ticker timer with tape (or light gates), identical elastic bands (or slotted masses on a string over a pulley), metre rule. Procedure: tilt the runway slightly so the trolley runs at constant speed (this compensates for friction). Pull the trolley with one elastic band kept at a constant stretch and find the acceleration from the ticker tape. Repeat with 2, 3, 4 and 5 bands side by side (forces F, 2F, 3F…), keeping the mass the same. Results: plot acceleration against force; a straight line through the origin shows a ∝ F. Precautions: compensate for friction, keep the mass constant, keep the bands at the same stretch, repeat each reading and take an average.

Include a labelled diagram: the runway on a small slope, ticker timer at the top, tape attached to the trolley, and the elastic band(s) pulling the trolley. Acceleration from the tape: a = (v − u) / t, using the spacing of the dots in equal time intervals.

7b(i)Mass vs weight
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Mass is the quantity of matter in a body and is the same everywhere (unit: kg); weight is the gravitational force on the body and changes from place to place (unit: N).

Also accepted: mass is a scalar, weight is a vector; mass is measured with a beam balance, weight with a spring balance.

7b(ii)Weight on the other planet
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 325 N

Mass = W ÷ g = 500 ÷ 10 = 50 kg (mass does not change). Weight on the planet = mg = 50 × 6.5 = 325 N.

7c(i)Moment of a force
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The moment of a force about a point is the force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force.

Moment = F × d, measured in N m.

7c(ii)Conditions for equilibrium
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 1. The resultant force on the body is zero (upward forces = downward forces). 2. The sum of clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments (principle of moments).

The first condition stops the body moving along a line; the second stops it turning.

7c(iii)Force diagram of the bar
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Draw the bar from 0 cm to 100 cm with the pivot at 30 cm. Mark: the upward reaction R at the pivot; the load X acting down at the 0 cm end, 30 cm from the pivot; the weight of the bar, 200 N, acting down at its centre (50 cm mark), 20 cm from the pivot.

A uniform bar's weight acts at its midpoint. Weight = mg = 20 × 10 = 200 N.

7c(iv)Weight of load X
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: X ≈ 133 N

Take moments about the pivot: anticlockwise moment = clockwise moment, so X × 30 = 200 × 20. X = 4000 ÷ 30 = 133.3 N.

7d(i)Specific heat capacity
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The specific heat capacity of a substance is the quantity of heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of the substance by 1 K (or 1 °C).

Unit: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Formula: Q = mcΔθ.

7d(ii)Experiment: specific heat capacity of aluminium
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Apparatus: aluminium block of known mass m with two holes, immersion heater, thermometer, power supply, ammeter, voltmeter (or a joulemeter), stopwatch, lagging. Procedure: put the heater and the thermometer in the holes (a drop of oil in the thermometer hole) and wrap the block in lagging. Record the starting temperature θ₁. Switch on, start the stopwatch and record V and I. After time t, switch off and record the highest temperature θ₂. Calculation: heat supplied = VIt = mc(θ₂ − θ₁), so c = VIt ÷ [m(θ₂ − θ₁)]. Precautions: lag the block to reduce heat loss, use oil for good thermal contact, read the highest temperature reached after switching off, keep V and I steady.

Include a labelled diagram: the lagged block with the heater and thermometer in it, and the heater connected to the supply with an ammeter in series and a voltmeter in parallel.

7e(i)Heat supplied by the heater
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 600 000 J (600 kJ)

Q = Pt = 500 W × (20 × 60 s) = 500 × 1200 = 600 000 J.

7e(ii)Temperature change of the water
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: About 28.6 K (28.6 °C)

Q = mcΔθ, so Δθ = Q ÷ (mc) = 600 000 ÷ (5 × 4200) = 600 000 ÷ 21 000 = 28.6 K (assuming no heat is lost).

7e(iii)Water in car radiators
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Water has a high specific heat capacity, so it absorbs a large amount of heat from the engine with only a small rise in its own temperature.

It is also cheap, easily available and flows easily round the engine.

7f(i)Definition of temperature
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

It shows the direction in which heat flows: from a body at higher temperature to one at lower temperature.

7f(ii)Properties of mercury for thermometers
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Any two: it is opaque and shiny, so it is easy to see; it expands uniformly; it is a good conductor of heat, so it responds quickly; it stays liquid over a wide range (−39 °C to 357 °C); it does not wet the glass.

Each correct property earns one mark.

7f(iii)Temperature of the liquid
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 62.5 °C

Ice point = 10 cm (0 °C), steam point = 50 cm (100 °C). θ = (35 − 10) ÷ (50 − 10) × 100 = 25 ÷ 40 × 100 = 62.5 °C.

8a(i)Ohm's law
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The current flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided the temperature and other physical conditions stay constant.

V ∝ I, so V / I = R (a constant).

8a(ii)Graph of V against I
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: V on the y-axis and I on the x-axis, with a sensible scale that uses most of the graph paper, both axes labelled with units, all eight points plotted accurately, and a single straight line of best fit through the origin.

Marks are usually given for the title, the axes and units, the scale, the plotted points and the best-fit line.

8a(iii)Gradient and its meaning
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Gradient ≈ 1.4 Ω (accept about 1.38 to 1.42). It is the resistance of the conductor.

Use a large triangle on your line, e.g. from (0, 0) to (7.1 A, 10.0 V): gradient = ΔV / ΔI = 10.0 ÷ 7.1 ≈ 1.4 V A⁻¹ = 1.4 Ω. Since V = IR, the gradient V/I is R.

8a(iv)Current when V = 7.5 V
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: About 5.3 A (accept 5.2 to 5.4 A)

Draw a horizontal line from 7.5 V on the V-axis to your graph line, then a vertical line down to the I-axis and read the value. Check: I = V / R = 7.5 ÷ 1.4 ≈ 5.3 A. Show the dashed lines on your graph.

8b(i)Electromotive force
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The e.m.f. of a source is the energy it converts into electrical energy per unit charge (per coulomb) that passes through it, driving the charge round a complete circuit.

Unit: volt (1 V = 1 J C⁻¹). It equals the p.d. across the terminals when no current flows.

8b(ii)Effect of a thinner wire
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The current in the ammeter decreases.

R = ρL / A. A thinner wire has a smaller cross-sectional area A, so its resistance is greater. With the same battery, a greater resistance gives a smaller current (I = V / R).

8c(i)Function and position of a fuse
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: A fuse protects the appliance and the wiring from too large a current. It is connected in series in the live wire.

In the live wire, a blown fuse disconnects the appliance from the high voltage.

8c(ii)How a fuse works
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The fuse is a short, thin wire with a low melting point. When the current is greater than its rating, the wire heats up, melts and breaks the circuit, so the current stops.

This is the heating effect of a current (power = I²R).

8d(i)Snell's law
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: For light passing from one medium to another, the ratio sin i / sin r is a constant (the refractive index). The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.

The constant is n, the refractive index of the second medium relative to the first.

8d(ii)Graph of real depth against apparent depth
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Real depth on the y-axis and apparent depth on the x-axis, with a sensible scale, axes labelled with units (cm), all points plotted accurately, and a straight line of best fit through the origin.

Marks are usually given for the title, the axes and units, the scale, the plotted points and the best-fit line.

8d(iii)Gradient and its meaning
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Gradient ≈ 1.3 (no unit; accept about 1.28 to 1.34). It is the refractive index of water.

Use a large triangle on your line, e.g. from (0, 0) to (15.4 cm, 20.0 cm): gradient = 20.0 ÷ 15.4 ≈ 1.3. Refractive index = real depth ÷ apparent depth. (The accepted value for water is 1.33.)

8d(iv)Apparent position for 14.0 cm of water
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: About 10.7 cm below the water surface (accept 10.5 to 10.9 cm).

Draw a horizontal line from 14.0 cm on the real-depth axis to your line, then a vertical line down to the apparent-depth axis and read the value. Check: apparent depth = real depth ÷ n = 14.0 ÷ 1.31 ≈ 10.7 cm. So the coin appears about 10.7 cm below the surface, about 3.3 cm higher than it really is.

8e(i)Total internal reflection
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Total internal reflection happens when light travelling in a denser medium strikes the boundary with a less dense medium at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle, so all of the light is reflected back into the denser medium.

No light is refracted out at all.

8e(ii)Conditions for total internal reflection
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 1. Light must travel from an optically denser medium to a less dense one (e.g. glass to air). 2. The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.

Both conditions are needed.

8f(i)A member of the electromagnetic spectrum
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Example: X-rays, wavelength about 10⁻¹¹ m to 10⁻⁸ m.

Any member with a correct range is accepted, e.g. infrared about 7 × 10⁻⁷ m to 10⁻³ m, or ultraviolet about 10⁻⁸ m to 4 × 10⁻⁷ m.

8f(ii)A use linked to its property
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: X-rays are used in hospitals to take images of bones, because they pass easily through soft tissue (flesh) but are absorbed by dense bone, which leaves a shadow on the film.

Link the use to a property. For infrared: TV remote controls or cooking/heating, because infrared has a heating effect and can be sent as invisible beams.

9a(i)Radioactive decay
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Radioactive decay is the spontaneous and random breakdown of an unstable nucleus, which gives out alpha, beta or gamma radiation to become more stable.

It cannot be sped up or slowed down by heat, pressure or chemical change.

9a(ii)Completing the radiation table
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Alpha: range in air a few cm (about 3 to 5 cm); stopped by a sheet of paper. Beta: deflected towards the positive plate (more strongly than alpha, in the opposite direction); stopped by a few mm of aluminium. Gamma: range in air very long (several hundred metres, it is never completely stopped); not deflected by an electric field.

Alpha particles are positive (helium nuclei), so they go towards the negative plate. Beta particles are fast electrons, so they go towards the positive plate. Gamma rays are electromagnetic waves with no charge, so they are not deflected.

9b(i)Half-life
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: The half-life of a radioactive substance is the time taken for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay (or for its count rate/activity to fall to half).

Each nuclide has its own constant half-life.

9b(ii)Half-life of the carbon-14 sample
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 5800 years

1000 → 500 → 250 counts min⁻¹ is 2 half-lives. 2 half-lives = 11 600 years, so 1 half-life = 11 600 ÷ 2 = 5800 years.

9b(iii)Protons and neutrons in carbon-14
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 6 protons and 8 neutrons.

Proton number Z = 6. Neutrons = nucleon number − proton number = 14 − 6 = 8.

9b(iv)Neutron to proton ratio of carbon-14
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: n : p = 8 : 6 = 1.33 (4 : 3). It is unstable.

Light nuclei are stable when the ratio is about 1 (e.g. carbon-12: 6 : 6). Carbon-14 has too many neutrons, so it is unstable and radioactive (it decays by beta emission).

9c(i)Health hazards of radiation
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Any two: cancer (e.g. leukaemia); radiation burns and radiation sickness; damage to cells and genes, causing mutations and birth defects in children; sterility.

Radiation ionises the molecules in body cells and damages them.

9c(ii)Still hazardous after a few weeks?
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Yes, they would still be hazardous.

The isotope has a long half-life, so after a few weeks almost none of it has decayed: its activity is nearly the same as at the start. It keeps emitting radiation and can get into the air, water, soil and food, where people breathe it in or eat it.

9c(iii)Radiation detector
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: A Geiger-Müller tube with a counter (G-M counter).

Also accepted: cloud chamber, spark counter, photographic film (badge).

9d(i)Law of conservation of energy
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be changed from one form to another.

The total energy of a closed system stays the same.

9d(ii)Completing the energy-conversion table
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Microphone: sound energy → electrical energy. Electrical energy → heat: an electric heater (or electric iron, kettle or cooker). Stretched rubber band released to throw a stone: elastic (strain) potential energy → kinetic energy.

A microphone is a transducer that changes sound vibrations into electrical signals. A heater changes electrical energy into heat through its resistance. A stretched band stores elastic energy, which becomes the motion energy of the stone.

9e(i)Definition of power
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Power is the rate of doing work (or of transferring energy).

P = work ÷ time; unit: watt (1 W = 1 J s⁻¹).

9e(ii)Energy supplied to the motor
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 3960 J

E = VIt = 220 V × 3 A × 6 s = 3960 J.

9e(iii)Work done lifting the load
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 3000 J

W = force × distance = 600 N × 5 m = 3000 J.

9e(iv)Is the motor 100% efficient?
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: No. Its efficiency is about 76% (3000 ÷ 3960 × 100).

The useful work output (3000 J) is less than the energy input (3960 J). The rest (960 J) is wasted as heat in the coils, friction in the motor and pulleys, and sound.

9f(i)Hazards of careless use of electricity
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: Any two: electric shock (which can kill); fire caused by overloading or short circuits; damage to appliances.

For example: wet hands on switches, bare or damaged cables, too many plugs on one socket.

9f(ii)Potential energy of the water
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: 375 000 J (375 kJ)

PE = mgh = 500 kg × 10 N kg⁻¹ × 75 m = 375 000 J.

9f(iii)Less electricity in the dry season
🤖 Ask AI tutor

Answer: In the dry season there is less water in the dam: the water level (height) is lower and less water falls on the turbines each second.

PE = mgh: with a smaller mass of water per second and a smaller height, less potential energy (and so less kinetic energy) reaches the turbines, so less electrical power is produced.
